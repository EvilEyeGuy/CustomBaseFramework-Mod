Material lists

Loot

Material lists give a settlement a consistent set of construction choices: walls, doors, gates, fences, roads, bridges and field surfaces. Each role can try several candidates in order, allowing a preferred modded object to fall back to an available alternative.

Each such part is a role, for example Wall or Door. A piece can also name a role instead of a fixed thing. The material list then decides what fills the role, so the same piece can look different in different settlement types.

Every settlement type names one material list. There is no hidden default: a settlement type that names no material list is switched off.

The lists are edited in the Lists tab, part Materials.

Change the roads without rebuilding the houses

Create a material list that inherits from the one your plan already uses. Add or override the Road role, choose its terrain, and select the new list in the plan's General section.

Check the ward's Paths setting too: an explicit ward surface takes precedence over the default road material. Fixed floors drawn inside a piece also keep their own definitions. This lets you change the streets while leaving furnished interiors intact.

A list can inherit from another list, set on the right under Inherits from (the parent). Roles the list does not define itself come from the parent. The same happens when none of the list's own rows for a role fit. Inherited roles are shown greyed under Inherited. Override copies them into the list so you can change them.

Roles

Each row is one role. A role can appear in several rows with different conditions, see Which row wins.

Role Used for
Wall The town wall and towers where the generator builds them itself. Walls of pieces that name the role.
Inner wall Walls between rooms, in pieces that name the role.
Door Doors in the town wall, on the walkway, in towers and posterns.
Gate Gates in wall passages without a gatehouse.
Embrasure Embrasures in the town wall.
Pillar, Window Pillars and windows in pieces that name the role.
Road Roads, when the ward names no roads of its own.
Road edge The strip along roads, in pieces that name the role. The generator takes road edges from the ward.
Interior floor, Fine floor, Rough floor, Dirt floor Floors the generator lays, for example fine floor on squares, rough floor under the town wall, dirt under fences. Floors of pieces that name the role.
Field soil, Crop Soil and plants of fields.
Bridge Bridges over water.
Roof, Thin roof Roofs of pieces that name the role.
Fence, Fence gate, Pen marker Pens for animals.
Light, Furniture, Container Things in pieces that name the role.
Conduit, Conduit under roads, Conduit under water Power lines of modern towns.

If no row gives a Door or an Embrasure, the generator builds wall there. Without a Fence gate, a pen stays open at that spot and the animals can walk off.

For each row:

  • Thing, Stuff, Floor, Roof: candidates in order. The first one that exists in the game is used, so a row can name a mod item first and a base game item after it. Stuff can also be Stone of the map (blocks from the rock of the map tile) or Wood of the map. In XML these are MapStone and MapWood. If no stuff exists, the thing's default stuff is used.
  • Applies to: pieces or categories. The row then only applies in a ward whose walls, towers or gatehouses use one of them. Example: a palisade door for wards fortified with palisade walls, palisade towers and palisade gates.
  • Ward: the row only applies in this kind of ward: Town, Suburb or Countryside. Example: cobblestone roads in the town, dirt roads in the countryside. A ward is one ring of a settlement, see Wards.
  • Critical or Optional: if a critical role finds nothing, the whole list is unusable and shown in red. An optional role simply stays empty.
  • Floor mix mixes several floors by weight. The weights are set in the file.
  • Tag search is the last resort: when no candidate exists, CBF looks for a thing or floor with this tag.

Applies to and Ward only change what is filled through the material list: the parts the generator draws itself, and roles a piece names. Things drawn directly into a piece stay as drawn.

Which row wins

When several rows of the same role fit, the most specific one wins. A row whose Applies to matches beats a row with a matching Ward. A row with a matching Ward beats a row without conditions. A row with both conditions must satisfy both and wins over a row with only one. At equal specificity, the first usable row wins. CBF searches the child list before falling back to its parent.

In the settlement type

The General section of the Settlement tab has the row Material list. The row turns red when the settlement type names no list or an unusable one. The ... button opens the list.

For writing material lists in XML, see Your own content mod.
