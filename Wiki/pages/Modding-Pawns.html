Modding: pawns, garrisons and unit swap

Modding

Configure settlement inhabitants in three stages: define pawn kinds, group them into weighted lists, then assign those lists to garrisons or anchors. Tech filtering selects suitable members, and unit swaps adapt them to the owning faction. The editor exposes the same data through Pawns, Unit swap and Settlements.

For editor terminology, see the guide overview. To change an existing definition, use a patch; the XML below explains the underlying data.

Who lives in a settlement

A CBF settlement gets its pawns from four sources.

  • Garrison per ward. Each ward can name pawn lists with a count and a post. These are the soldiers of that ward.
  • Defender points. Wards without a garrison get pawns bought with the settlement type's defenderPoints. Those pawns come from the faction's own pawn group makers, the same way vanilla generates a faction base.
  • Pawn anchors in pieces. A piece can mark cells where pawns stand: a guard in a barracks, a trader at a stall, a crew at a catapult.
  • Pen animals. A pen names a pawn list of animals.

Humanlike pawns from garrisons and anchors pass through unit swap before they spawn. A neutral archer in a list can therefore become the archer of the faction that owns the settlement.

Pawns are only placed for settlements of a non-player faction.

Pawn lists

A pawn list is a weighted list of pawn kinds with a role and a radius. Garrisons, pawn anchors, pens and object lists all refer to pawn lists by defName. One list can be used in many places.

Example

From 1.6/Defs/CustomBaseFramework/LootDefs/CBF_PawnLists.xml:

<CustomBaseFramework.PawnListDef>
  <defName>CBF_Vanilla_Garrison</defName>
  <label>garrison</label>
  <role>Guard</role>
  <radius>10</radius>
  <members>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Rifleman</kind><weight>4</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Stormer</kind><weight>2</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Sniper</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Grenadier</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Officer</kind><weight>1</weight><ideoRole>Kampf</ideoRole></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Spacer_Rifleman</kind><weight>4</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Spacer_Sniper</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Spacer_Stormer</kind><weight>2</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Spacer_Grenadier</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li><kind>CBF_Modern_Spacer_Officer</kind><weight>1</weight><ideoRole>Kampf</ideoRole></li>
  </members>
</CustomBaseFramework.PawnListDef>

A list that depends on another mod uses MayRequire, on the whole def, on single members, or on both. This list is shipped in the same file:

<CustomBaseFramework.PawnListDef MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul">
  <defName>CBF_MO_Guard</defName>
  <label>guard</label>
  <role>Guard</role>
  <radius>4</radius>
  <members>
    <li MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul"><kind>DankPyon_Medieval_Arbalester</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul"><kind>DankPyon_Medieval_Bowman</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul"><kind>CBF_MO_Pikeman</kind><weight>1</weight></li>
    <li MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul"><kind>CBF_MO_Knight</kind><weight>1</weight><ideoRole>Kampf</ideoRole></li>
  </members>
</CustomBaseFramework.PawnListDef>

Nodes

members holds the entries. Each li has:

  • kind: defName of a PawnKindDef. A kind that is not loaded is skipped without a message.
  • weight: how often this kind is drawn compared to the others. Default 1. A weight of 0 is never drawn.
  • ideoRole: optional, needs Ideology. Pawns of this entry get a role of their faction's ideoligion. Kampf gives the shooting specialist or the melee specialist, depending on the pawn's weapon. Any other value is the defName of a role precept. An unknown role logs a warning and the entry gets no role.

role decides how pawns from this list behave when they come from a pawn anchor. Default Civilian. The editor label is shown in the second column.

Value Editor label Behavior at a pawn anchor
Guard Guard Joins the defense of the ward the anchor stands in.
Sentry Post Holds a post around the anchor within radius.
Gunner Gunner Mans artillery around the anchor within radius. Crews stay at their gun.
Civilian Civilian Defends the area within radius, but may flee on its own. Need not be capable of violence.

In a garrison the role only decides one thing: pawns of every role except Civilian are generated capable of violence. Where garrison pawns stand is decided by the post of the garrison entry.

radius: how far, in cells, pawns from an anchor spread around it. Default 4. Values below 1 count as 1. Guard pawns ignore it because they join the ward's defense.

Tech level of the settlement

One list can hold soldiers of several ages, for example industrial and spacer troops. CBF then picks the members that fit the settlement.

A settlement has a tech level. It is the tech level of the owning faction if the settlement type lists that level in techLevels. Otherwise it is the highest level in techLevels. A settlement type without techLevels has no tech level.

CBF works out a tech level for each pawn kind from its gear: required apparel, required implants, and the weapons its weaponTags and weaponMoney allow. The highest tech level found counts.

When a garrison or a pawn anchor draws from a list:

  1. Members above the settlement's tech level are left out.
  2. Of the rest, only the members with the highest tech level stay in the pool.
  3. Members whose tech level cannot be determined always stay in the pool.
  4. One kind is drawn from the pool by weight.

If no member fits, the whole list is used and the log gets a warning. Without a settlement tech level the whole list is used.

In the example above, an industrial settlement draws only the industrial CBF_Modern_* soldiers. A spacer settlement draws only the CBF_Modern_Spacer_* kinds.

Pens and object lists draw from the whole list without this filter.

techLevel on the pawn list switches the filter off. Set it to a tech level such as Neolithic, and the list counts as that level: garrisons and pawn anchors draw from the whole list. Use it when the gear gives the wrong level, for example tribal warriors wearing medieval leather. If techLevel is above the settlement's tech level, the list is still used and the log gets a warning.

Checks

These problems are reported when the game loads:

  • A list without members is a config error.
  • A negative weight or radius is a config error.
  • A list whose members are all missing logs a warning. Anchors that name it stay empty.
  • A Civilian list that contains a humanlike kind with a combat power of 80 or more logs a warning. Fighters belong in Guard, Sentry or Gunner.

Garrisons

A garrison is a fixed set of soldiers for one ward. It is written in the ward entry inside wards of a SettlementPlanDef. Use a garrison when you want to control exactly who defends a ward and where they stand.

Example

From 1.6/Mods/MedievalOverhaul/Defs/CustomBaseFramework/PlanDefs/CBF_Plan_FortressCity_New.xml, shortened:

<CustomBaseFramework.SettlementPlanDef MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul">
  <defName>CBF_Plan_FortressCity_New</defName>
  <label>fortress city (MO)</label>
  <defenderPoints>400~700</defenderPoints>
  <defendersOnWall>0.28</defendersOnWall>
  <wards>
    <li>
      <kind>Town</kind>
      <name>OldTown</name>
      <garrison>
        <li>
          <list>CBF_MO_Garrison_Walkway</list>
          <count>16~21</count>
          <post>Walkway</post>
        </li>
        <li>
          <list>CBF_MO_Garrison_Gate</list>
          <count>5~8</count>
          <post>Gate</post>
        </li>
        <li>
          <list>CBF_MO_Garrison_Inside</list>
          <count>8~13</count>
          <post>Inside</post>
        </li>
      </garrison>
    </li>
    <li>
      <kind>Rural</kind>
      <name>Countryside</name>
      <garrison>
        <li>
          <list>CBF_MO_Garrison_Inside</list>
          <count>3~5</count>
          <post>Inside</post>
        </li>
      </garrison>
    </li>
  </wards>
</CustomBaseFramework.SettlementPlanDef>

Nodes

Each li in garrison has:

  • list: defName of a pawn list. A missing list logs a warning and the entry stays empty.
  • count: how many pawns, as a range. Default 1.
  • post: where they stand. Default Inside.
Value Editor label Where the pawns go
Inside Inside Streets, squares and yards of the ward. They defend the ward together.
Walkway Wall Wall walk cells next to an open embrasure (a gap in the wall to shoot through). Each pawn guards its own stretch of wall.
Gate Gate Gate cells. Each pawn holds a post at the gate.

If a ward has no wall walk at an open embrasure, Walkway pawns stand inside instead. The same applies to Gate when the ward has no gate cells.

Garrison and defender points

  • A ward with at least one garrison entry gets no pawns from the defender points. Its garrison and its anchor pawns defend it.
  • The pawns bought with defenderPoints are split among the wards without a garrison, weighted by their size. Of each share, the fraction defendersOnWall goes onto the wall walk, if the ward has open embrasures.
  • The defender pawns come from the faction's pawn group maker named in defenderGroupKind. If that is empty or the faction has none of that kind, CBF tries Settlement, then Combat.
  • Pawns from anchors that name their own list or kind come on top. They are not counted against the defender points.

The editor shows a point value for each garrison entry: the weighted average combat power of the list, times the middle of count. This value is for orientation only.

On a quest site whose ruin uses occupation Occupier, garrisons and anchors with their own list or kind are skipped. Only defender points are used there. See Modding-Quests.

Lords

Every ward defends itself. Each garrison pawn on the wall or at a gate gets its own duty. All other defenders of a ward share one lord for that ward: inside garrison, defenders from points, and Guard pawns from anchors. They do not gather in the center of the settlement.

Pawn anchors in pieces

A pawn anchor places pawns at a spot inside a piece. It is a legend entry of a PieceDef with anchorKind set to Spawn. Its symbol is drawn in the piece's anchors grid. Neighboring cells with the same symbol form one anchor. The pawns spawn around a cell in the middle of that group. See Modding-Pieces for anchors in general.

Example

From the vanilla barracks piece CBF_Vanilla_Barracks_A:

<legend>
  <li><symbol>8</symbol><anchor>Commander</anchor><anchorKind>Spawn</anchorKind><pawnList>CBF_Vanilla_Garrison</pawnList><pawnCount>1</pawnCount></li>
  <li><symbol>k</symbol><anchor>SoldiersA</anchor><anchorKind>Spawn</anchorKind><pawnList>CBF_Vanilla_Garrison</pawnList><pawnCount>2~3</pawnCount></li>
</legend>

A house with residents:

<li><symbol>Y</symbol><anchor>Spawn</anchor><anchorKind>Spawn</anchorKind><pawnList>CBF_Vanilla_Villager</pawnList></li>

Nodes

  • anchor: name of the anchor. It appears in log messages.
  • anchorKind: Spawn for a pawn anchor.
  • pawnList: defName of a pawn list. The list's tech filter, role and radius apply.
  • pawns: defName of a single pawn kind. Used when pawnList is empty. These pawns are always capable of violence and join the defense of their ward.
  • pawnCount: how many pawns, as a range. Default 1. A count below 1 counts as 1.
  • chance: chance that the anchor is used at all, rolled once per anchor. Default 1.

If an anchor names neither pawnList nor pawns, it takes pawns from the settlement's defender points. It stays empty if no defender points were rolled, for example when every ward has a garrison. It also stays empty if it lies in a room without a way outside.

If pawnList names a list that does not exist and pawns is empty, the anchor stays empty and the log names it.

Anchor limits

Big settlements can contain many pieces with anchors. Two fields of the SettlementPlanDef cap the pawns from anchors:

  • maxCivilians: default 30. Once this many non-fighters are placed, further civilian anchors are skipped.
  • maxSoldiers: default 40. Once this many fighters are placed, further fighting anchors are skipped. Gunner anchors are never skipped.

Garrison pawns placed before count toward these limits. When a limit is active, anchors are handled in random order, gunner anchors first. 0 removes a limit.

Single pawns in the legend

A normal legend entry (not an anchor) can carry pawnKind instead of thing. It places one pawn of that kind on each cell with the symbol. The pawn belongs to the settlement's faction and passes through unit swap. It gets no role, radius or lord from CBF. For defenders, use a pawn anchor instead.

Pawns in containers

An object list (LootBuildingCategoryDef) decides which container stands at a Fill anchor. It can also put pawns into its containers with pawnList, pawnChance, pawnCount, pawnHostile and pawnFaction. These draw from the whole list without the tech filter. See Loot.

Pen animals

A pen is a fenced area in the countryside. It names a pawn list in pawns. The list should hold animals.

From a shipped camp plan:

<pens>
  <li>
    <name>Pen</name>
    <count>1~1</count>
    <herd>2~5</herd>
    <pawns>Hardanimals_1</pawns>
    <terrain>Soil</terrain>
    <plants>
      <li>Plant_Haygrass</li>
    </plants>
    <weight>1</weight>
  </li>
</pens>

Each pen draws one kind from the list and places herd animals of it. Small pens get fewer: at most one animal per twelve cells inside the fence, but every pen may hold at least one. The animals belong to the settlement's faction. If the drawn kind is not an animal, the pen stays empty and the log gets a warning. All pen nodes are described in Modding-Settlements.

Unit swap

Pawn lists and pieces name general units and things. A UnitSwapDef says what one faction uses instead. A noble house then sends its own knights and hangs its own banners, without a copy of each list and piece per faction.

Example

From 1.6/Defs/CustomBaseFramework/Einheitentausch/CBF_MO_House_Hesse.xml, shortened:

<CustomBaseFramework.UnitSwapDef MayRequire="DankPyon.Medieval.Overhaul">
  <defName>CBF_MO_House_Hesse</defName>
  <faction>DankPyon_NobleHouseSavage</faction>
  <units>
    <li><from>DankPyon_Amboisse_Knight</from><to>DankPyon_Hesse_Knight</to></li>
    <li><from>CBF_MO_Knight</from><to>DankPyon_KnightHesse</to></li>
    <li><from>CBF_MO_Pikeman</from><to>DankPyon_StandardHesse</to></li>
    <li><from>DankPyon_Medieval_Arbalester</from><to>DankPyon_Hesse_Archer</to></li>
    <li><from>DankPyon_Medieval_Arbalester</from><to>DankPyon_Medieval_Arbalester</to></li>
  </units>
  <things>
    <li><from>DankPyon_BannerAmboise</from><to>DankPyon_BannerHesse</to></li>
    <li><from>DankPyon_ShieldHeater_Amboise</from><to>DankPyon_ShieldHeater_Hesse</to></li>
  </things>
</CustomBaseFramework.UnitSwapDef>

The two lines for DankPyon_Medieval_Arbalester have the same weight. Half of the arbalesters become Hesse archers, the other half stay arbalesters.

Nodes

  • faction: defName of the FactionDef the rule is for.
  • priority: a whole number. Default 0. See below.
  • units: swaps for pawn kinds.
  • things: swaps for things placed by pieces.
  • neutral: pawn kinds that the automatic fallback must not replace. See below.

Each li in units and things has:

  • from: defName of the unit or thing named in the list or piece.
  • to: defName of what the faction uses instead.
  • weight: weight when one from has several targets. Default 1. Entries with 0 or less are ignored.

A def needs faction or neutral, otherwise it is a config error.

Priority

Several defs can bring rules for the same faction, also from different mods. For each from, only the entries with the highest priority count. Entries of equal priority from different defs are merged and drawn together by weight. To override the rule of another mod, give yours a higher priority.

A target that is not loaded is skipped. If no target of a rule is loaded, a unit is handled as if there were no rule, and a thing stays as it is.

The editor saves each rule as its own file in Defs/CustomBaseFramework/Einheitentausch of the chosen mod. Files of other mods are never changed.

Without a rule

When a faction has no rule for a humanlike unit, CBF picks a replacement itself:

  1. The candidates are the faction's own humanlike kinds: every kind in its pawn group makers (options, guards, traders, carriers) and its basicMemberKind.
  2. If the unit is already one of them, it stays.
  3. Only candidates with the same isFighter value as the unit are used.
  4. Candidates with the same way of fighting are preferred: ranged, melee or unarmed, judged by the weapons the kind can carry.
  5. Of those, the one with the closest combat power wins.

Animals and things are never swapped without a rule. The log notes each fallback swap once.

Neutral units

Units listed in neutral are never replaced by the fallback. Explicit rules for them still apply. CBF uses this for its own modern soldiers, so that every faction that owns a modern settlement keeps them. Shortened from CBF_Modern_Neutral_Units.xml:

<CustomBaseFramework.UnitSwapDef>
  <defName>CBF_Modern_Neutral_Units</defName>
  <label>neutral modern soldiers</label>
  <neutral>
    <li>CBF_Modern_Rifleman</li>
    <li>CBF_Modern_Sniper</li>
    <li>CBF_Modern_Stormer</li>
    <li>CBF_Modern_Spacer_Rifleman</li>
  </neutral>
</CustomBaseFramework.UnitSwapDef>

Where swap applies

  • Garrison pawns.
  • Pawns from pawn anchors, both pawnList and pawns.
  • Pawns from pawnKind in a legend entry.
  • Things placed by pieces, for things.

Defender pawns from defenderPoints are not swapped. They already come from the faction's own group makers. Pen animals are not swapped either.

Tips

  • Put all ages into one list and let the tech filter choose. A kind without required gear and without weapons has no tech level and is drawn at every level.
  • Use Civilian only for people who should run. Fighters in a civilian list trigger a warning.
  • Give each ward of a big settlement its own garrison. A ward with a garrison is fully under your control. A ward without one depends on the faction's group makers.
  • Wall posts need open embrasures. Without them the wall garrison stands inside.
  • Name general units in lists and pieces and write one swap rule per faction, instead of copying lists.
  • Put MayRequire on lists and rules for other mods, and on single members.
  • Techschritt has nothing to do with pawns or unit swap. It belongs to MapStepsDef and holds back a map generation step below a tech level.
