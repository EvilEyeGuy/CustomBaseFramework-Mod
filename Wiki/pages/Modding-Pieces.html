Pieces and categories in XML

Modding

A piece is one hand-drawn building or one stretch of town wall. CBF builds a settlement by choosing pieces and copying them onto the map. In XML a piece is a CustomBaseFramework.PieceDef. It holds a few character grids, one per layer (floor, things, roof and so on), and a legend that says what each character means.

A category (CustomBaseFramework.PieceCategoryDef) groups pieces, for example all farmsteads or all apothecaries. Settlement types order buildings by category, and the category carries the rules its pieces share. See Categories.

The editor writes piece files for you, see Pieces. This page is for reading and writing them by hand. All field names are the exact XML names. The full field list is on the reference pages at the end.

Terms used on this page:

  • Anchor: a marked area in a piece where something is added after the building stands, such as loot, an object, pawns, or a link to the town wall or an annex. See Anchors.
  • Object list (LootBuildingCategoryDef): the buildings an anchor may place, such as barrels or shelves. See Loot in XML.
  • Loot table (LootTableDef): decides which items go into or onto that object. See Loot in XML.
  • Pawn list (PawnListDef): a weighted list of pawn kinds. See Pawns in XML.
  • Annex: a small extra piece, such as a shed or privy, that docks onto a house. See Annexes.
  • Material list (MaterialListDef): turns building roles such as "wall" into real things for one settlement type. See Material lists.
  • Settlement type (SettlementPlanDef): the building program of a settlement. It is split into wards (rings such as the walled town and the countryside) and districts (groups of buildings inside a ward). See Settlement types in XML.

How to read it:

  • S in things is a granite wall, F a wooden door. The upper room is a kitchen with a table and chairs, the lower left room a bedroom.
  • B in things2 lays a hidden conduit under the walls.
  • w and h in terrain are wood plank and granite tile floor.
  • ^ in roof puts a roof over the whole house.
  • In anchors, 1 marks the kitchen shelf, which gets kitchen loot. 2 marks the bedroom shelf, which gets household loot. 8 marks a spot where a villager may stand, with a chance of 0.3.

The shortened legend leaves out E, L, R, g and j. In the real file they are an end table, a lamp, two more chairs, a stove and a table.

The grids

Every grid is a list of rows. One character is one cell.

  • The first row is the north edge of the piece. The first character of a row is the west edge.
  • . and a space mean an empty cell. Nothing is done there on that layer.
  • Every other character is looked up in the legend. In the content grid it is looked up in contentZones instead.
  • All grids of a piece should have the same number of rows and the same row length. The load check reports it when they do not (the two anchor grids are not checked).
  • The size of the piece is the largest grid. There is no separate size field.
Grid What it holds
mask The footprint: which cells belong to the piece. Any character other than . or a space counts. The generator plans with this shape. With clearFootprint on, only these cells are cleared before building. Without a mask, the generator takes the shape from the terrain grid, and clearing uses every cell that is filled on any layer.
terrain The floor.
things The main layer: walls, doors, furniture, everything a cell is built from. The generator reads this layer (and things2) to tell walls from doors, see below.
things2 A second layer for things that share a cell with the first, for example a conduit under a wall or a torch on it.
things3 A third layer for the rare cell that carries three things.
plants Plants. They get a random growth and are skipped on water and on cells with a building.
roof Where a roof goes.
anchors Anchor cells, see Anchors.
anchorsOver A second anchor grid. Fill and Object anchors here place their objects on top of what already stands, without clearing the cells.
content Old loot zones, resolved through contentZones. New pieces use Fill anchors instead.

In the house above, the conduit B in things2 runs under the walls S of things. With a single layer the wall would need a gap for it.

The generator decides which cells are walls and doors from things, then things2:

  • A door: the entry has the role DOOR or GATE, or the thing's defName contains door or gate (any case).
  • A wall: the role WALL or PILLAR, or a defName that contains wall (but not torch or lamp) or palisade. In a WallPart piece every thing counts as wall.

A normal building without any door cell and without a Wall anchor is treated as open: it can be entered from every side. A warning is logged for it.

The legend

Each <li> in legend gives one character a meaning. symbol must be exactly one character.

Field Effect
thing A ThingDef placed on this cell. A thing larger than one cell is placed once, from the cell you mark.
stuff The material. If it does not fit the thing, the thing's default material is used and a warning is logged.
rotation North, East, South or West. Default North. It turns with the piece.
terrain A TerrainDef for the floor layer.
roof A RoofDef for the roof layer. A roof cell whose entry names no roof (and no role that gives one) gets RoofConstructed.
pawnKind A PawnKindDef. A pawn of the settlement's faction is spawned on this cell.
role A building role such as WALL or DOOR instead of a fixed def. CBF takes the thing from the material list of the settlement type, see Roles.
chance Chance from 0 to 1. On things, floors, roofs and plants it is rolled per cell.
count Stack size for a stackable thing, as a range such as 5~20.
temperature Target temperature for a cooler or heater, in degrees Celsius.
biomeLeather true replaces the material with the leather of a wild animal of the map's biome, chosen once per piece.
variants Alternative things or floors for this character, see below.
anchor and the fields after it Make the entry an anchor, see Anchors.

An entry must have at least one of role, thing, terrain, pawnKind, roof or anchor. Otherwise the load check reports it.

Two things change in the game, not in the file. Stone blocks as material are swapped for a stone that exists on the map tile. Floors named after a stone, such as TileGranite, are swapped the same way.

A shipped example for temperature:

<li><symbol>K</symbol><thing>Cooler</thing><rotation>South</rotation><temperature>-19</temperature></li>

Variants

variants lets one character stand for several things or floors. Each cell picks one by weight, from the cell position and the settlement seed.

<li>
  <symbol>w</symbol>
  <terrain>WoodPlankFloor</terrain>
  <variants>
    <li><terrain>TileGranite</terrain><weight>0.5</weight></li>
    <li><terrain>CarpetRed</terrain><weight>0.5</weight></li>
  </variants>
</li>
  • A variant takes thing, stuff, terrain and weight. Default weight is 1.
  • If the entry itself names a thing or terrain, that counts as one more choice with weight 1. Here the planks get half of the cells.
  • A variant's stuff is only used together with its thing.
  • Variants apply to the terrain grid and the three thing grids, not to plants.

This example is written for this page, it is not from a shipped piece.

Piece fields

Field Effect
schemaVersion The file format. The current version is 2. Older files are upgraded on load.
categories The categories of the piece. The first is the primary one. When two categories disagree, the first category wins, then its parents, then the next category.
tag The old single category. Still read when categories is missing. Write categories.
type Building (default), WallPart or DungeonRoom. Gatehouses and wall segments are WallPart.
template true makes the piece a copy source for the editor. It is never placed.
allowRotation Default true. With false the piece is always built as drawn.
allowMirror Default true. Mirroring flips the piece east to west.
clearFootprint Default true. Removes what stands on the piece's cells before building. Water cells and pawns are left alone.
placement Overrides the category rules for this one piece, see below.
onlyWithMod packageIds, separated by commas. The piece is only used when all of them are active.
offIfMod packageIds, separated by commas. The piece is not used when one of them is active.
drawnOutward For wall pieces without an Outside anchor: the side that faces out of town, see Connect.
style Old field. It does not restrict where the piece is used.
eaves Places eaves from Architecture of the Rim on outer walls and doors. Does nothing without that mod.
eavesMaterial Material of the eaves. Empty takes the material of the wall they sit on.

placement

<placement><atWall>Preferred</atWall></placement>
<placement><weight>0.3</weight></placement>
  • atWall says how much the piece wants to touch the town wall: Forbidden, Avoid, Allowed, Preferred, StronglyPreferred or Required. Default is Allowed. When it is Allowed or missing, wallContact of the category decides.
  • weight says how often this piece is picked over others that fit. It falls back to the category weight, then 1.
  • factions and excludeFactions list FactionDef defNames. With factions the piece is only used in settlements of those factions. With excludeFactions it is never used in theirs.
  • withIdeo and withoutIdeo do the same for memes and precepts of the owning faction's ideoligion (needs Ideology).
  • All four lists can also sit on the category and are inherited. A piece that does not fit counts as missing for this settlement. If a plan orders it by name and nothing else fits, the entry stays empty.
  • zones, needsRoadAccess and countPerSettlement are still accepted here, but the generator does not read them on the piece.
<placement>
  <factions>
    <li>DankPyon_NobleHouseFierce</li>
  </factions>
</placement>

Anchors

An anchor marks cells where something happens after the piece is built. An anchor is a legend entry with an anchor name. Its cells are drawn in the anchors grid with the entry's symbol.

  • Neighboring cells (north, east, south, west) with the same symbol form one anchor area.
  • anchorKind is Connect, Fill, Object or Spawn. Default is Connect.
  • The editor calls them Connect, Loot (Fill), Object and Pawn (Spawn).
  • chance on an anchor is rolled once per anchor area.

Connect

Connect anchors tie the piece to its surroundings. The name decides what for. Other names only label the anchor.

Name Meaning
Wall The town wall joins the piece here. A gatehouse has two, one on each side where the wall runs in. A normal building with a Wall anchor is built into the wall.
Outside Marks the side that faces out of town. A gatehouse uses it to know which way the gate faces.
Annex An annex docks here, see Annexes.
Water Harbor pieces only: the water side of a jetty, boat shed or fishing hut. Parts that stand over the water use bridge floor. See Settlement types in XML.

required is still read on a Wall anchor, but the current generator ignores it. To force a piece against the town wall, set atWall to Required in placement.

A gatehouse, simplified from CBF_Vanilla_Gatehouse_Wood:

<CustomBaseFramework.PieceDef>
  <defName>CBF_Vanilla_Gatehouse_Wood</defName>
  <schemaVersion>2</schemaVersion>
  <categories>
    <li>WallGateHouse</li>
    <li>CBF_Vanilla_Gatehouse</li>
  </categories>
  <type>WallPart</type>
  <legend>
    <li><symbol>7</symbol><anchor>GuardSouth</anchor><anchorKind>Spawn</anchorKind><pawnList>CBF_Vanilla_Garrison</pawnList><pawnCount>1~2</pawnCount></li>
    <li><symbol>A</symbol><anchor>Outside</anchor></li>
    <li><symbol>O</symbol><thing>Wall</thing><stuff>WoodLog</stuff></li>
    <li><symbol>W</symbol><anchor>Wall</anchor></li>
    <li><symbol>g</symbol><thing>Door</thing><stuff>WoodLog</stuff></li>
  </legend>
  <things>
    <li>OOOOO</li>
    <li>O...O</li>
    <li>OOOOO</li>
    <li>.g.g.</li>
    <li>OOOOO</li>
    <li>O...O</li>
    <li>OOOOO</li>
  </things>
  <anchors>
    <li>..W..</li>
    <li>.....</li>

    <li>..7..</li>

The real piece is 5 wide and 9 rows long and also has furniture, turrets, loot and a roof. Here the wall runs in at the north and south W. The gate passage is the row with the two doors g. A lies on the west side, so west is outside. The category WallGateHouse is what makes the generator treat the piece as a gatehouse.

If a wall piece has no Outside anchor, the field drawnOutward (North, East, South or West) names the outer side instead. Without both, the wall profile's setting applies, and south when there is none.

Fill

A Fill anchor places one object from an object list and fills it from a loot table.

<li><symbol>1</symbol><anchor>Kitchen</anchor><anchorKind>Fill</anchorKind><objects>CBF_Vanilla_Shelf_Small</objects><lootTable>CBF_Vanilla_Loot_Kitchen</lootTable></li>
  • objects names an object list (LootBuildingCategoryDef). One object that fits the area is placed.
  • lootTable names a loot table (LootTableDef). Without it the object stays empty, as decoration.
  • lootForeign set to true leaves the contents to the mod that brings the object. CBF adds no loot then.
  • Without an object list, a Fill anchor with a loot table puts the loot loose on the floor near the anchor.
  • Draw a placeholder under the anchor in the things grid, like the shelf a under anchor 1 in the house. The placed object takes the rotation and material of the placeholder. Without one, everything faces north.

See Loot in XML for object lists and loot tables.

Object

An Object anchor places an object from an object list, with the anchor's chance, and no loot.

<li><symbol>a</symbol><anchor>Dishes</anchor><anchorKind>Object</anchorKind><objects>CBF_MO_Tableware_Hut</objects><chance>0.8</chance><onTop>true</onTop></li>
  • onTop set to true keeps what already stands on the cells and places the object on top, here dishes on a table. Anchors in the anchorsOver grid always behave this way.
  • factionFlag set to true uses the flag object list that a FactionFlagDef assigns to the settlement's faction. Factions without one get the anchor's own objects.
  • ideoBuilding set to true places a building of the faction's ideoligion, such as an altar, if one fits the area. Otherwise the anchor's objects are used. Needs Ideology.

Spawn

A Spawn anchor marks where pawns wait when the settlement is manned.

<li><symbol>8</symbol><anchor>ResidentsA</anchor><anchorKind>Spawn</anchorKind><pawnList>CBF_Vanilla_Villager</pawnList><chance>0.3</chance></li>
  • pawnList names a pawn list (PawnListDef), see Pawns.
  • pawns names a single PawnKindDef instead. The editor calls it Animals.
  • pawnCount is the number of pawns, as a range. Default 1.

Roles

A legend entry can name a role instead of a fixed def. When the settlement is built, CBF takes the thing, floor or roof for that role from the material list of the settlement type, see Material lists.

<li><symbol>S</symbol><role>WALL</role></li>
<li><symbol>F</symbol><role>DOOR</role></li>
<li><symbol>w</symbol><role>FLOOR_INTERIOR</role></li>
  • The built-in roles are WALL, WALL_INNER, DOOR, GATE, PILLAR, WINDOW, EMBRASURE, FLOOR_INTERIOR, FLOOR_FINE, FLOOR_ROUGH, FLOOR_DIRT, FLOOR_ROAD, FLOOR_ROAD_EDGE, FLOOR_FIELD, BRIDGE, ROOF, ROOF_THIN, LIGHT, FURNITURE, CONTAINER, CROP, FENCE, FENCE_GATE, PEN_MARKER, CONDUIT, CONDUIT_ROAD and CONDUIT_WATER.
  • If the material list has no entry for the role, a thing cell stays empty and a warning is logged.
  • Cells with the role DOOR or GATE count as doors for the generator, see The grids.
  • Stone walls and floors take the stone of the map.

So one piece drawn with roles can be built in stone in one settlement type and in wood in another. The pieces shipped with CBF draw their things directly.

When the mod is missing, RimWorld drops those legend entries. CBF then checks which characters have no entry left:

  • A character in the things grid without an entry, outside any anchor cell, means the building would have holes. The piece is not used at all and a warning is logged once.
  • A character without an entry in terrain, roof, things2, things3, plants, or in things under an anchor, only leaves those cells empty. The piece is still used.

Objects placed through an object list are optional. Members whose thing does not exist are dropped. If the list ends up empty, the anchor stays empty and the rest of the piece is built.

The rule for your own pieces: draw walls, doors and everything the building needs directly. Put devices, furniture and decoration that only some mods bring into an object list behind an anchor.

The annex above also shows the Annex anchor. It lies on the west wall cell, so the annex shares its wall with the house. It could also lie one cell outside the shape. Then the annex stands next to the house with its own wall.

Categories

Two categories from the Medieval Overhaul set, shipped in 1.6/Defs/CustomBaseFramework/CBF_Freigabe_Kategorien.xml:

<CustomBaseFramework.PieceCategoryDef>
  <defName>CBF_MO_Farmstead</defName>
  <label>farmstead</label>
  <zones>
    <li>Outside</li>
  </zones>
  <needsRoadAccess>false</needsRoadAccess>
  <blockMax>1</blockMax>
  <annexes>
    <li>CBF_MO_Annex_Yard</li>
    <li>CBF_MO_Annex_Rural</li>
  </annexes>
  <annexMax>3</annexMax>
</CustomBaseFramework.PieceCategoryDef>

<CustomBaseFramework.PieceCategoryDef>
  <defName>CBF_MO_Apothecary</defName>
  <label>apothecary</label>
  <parent>CBF_MO_Workshop</parent>
  <zones>
    <li>Inside</li>
  </zones>
  <needsRoadAccess>true</needsRoadAccess>
  <annexes>
    <li>CBF_MO_Annex_Town</li>
  </annexes>
  <annexChance>0.5</annexChance>
  <annexMax>2</annexMax>
</CustomBaseFramework.PieceCategoryDef>

The farmstead needs no road and can be entered from every side (zones Outside with needsRoadAccess false). Farmsteads never stand wall to wall (blockMax 1). Each gets up to 3 annexes from two annex categories. The apothecary inherits from CBF_MO_Workshop and gets up to 2 town annexes, each tried with a chance of 0.5.

Inheritance

  • parent names the category this one inherits from. The chain may be up to 16 deep and must not loop.
  • A field that is not set takes the value of the parent, then its parent, and so on. Most category fields are optional for this reason.
  • A piece in a child category also counts as a piece of every parent. A settlement that orders CBF_MO_Workshop can get a CBF_MO_Apothecary piece.
  • blockMax, merge, zones and needsRoadAccess are read on each category itself, not inherited.

Placement rules

Field Effect
weight How often pieces of this category are picked when several fit.
wallContact Pieces prefer to stand against the town wall. Used when the piece's atWall is Allowed or missing.
zones, needsRoadAccess Only work together, see below.
factions, excludeFactions Only in, or never in, settlements of these factions (FactionDef defNames).
withIdeo, withoutIdeo Only with, or never with, these memes or precepts in the owning faction's ideoligion.
wallAccess The pieces give access to the town wall, like a barracks built against it. The generator closes their open sides and counts their cells when it places wall doors.

A piece counts as open when one of its own categories has needsRoadAccess set to false and Outside in zones. An open piece needs no road and can be entered from every side, like a farm field. Otherwise these two fields have no effect. The editor shows this as Needs no road.

How many pieces a settlement gets comes from the settlement type. Where they stand comes from its wards and districts, see Settlements.

Blocks

  • blockMax is the largest number of buildings of this category that join into one block, wall to wall. -1 (the default) uses the general limit of 3. 0 means no limit, 1 keeps them apart.
  • merge set to true lets neighbors of this category share walls anywhere. false forbids it. Without merge they only share walls where the plan is dense. Two buildings only merge when their primary category is the same.

Annexes

Field Set on Effect
annexes the house category Which categories may dock on as annexes. Empty takes the list of the parent.
annexChance the house category Chance per Annex anchor that an annex is tried. Default 0.6.
annexMax the house category Annexes per house at most. Default 2.
annexMultiple the annex category true allows the same annex more than once on one house.
annexFill the annex category true: the annex only docks where it covers the whole wall side of the house.
annexOverhang the annex category With annexFill: how many cells the annex must reach past the house wall at each end. 0 means flush.
annexCornerPath the annex category true turns the two inner corner wall cells at the ends of the house wall into floor where the annex docks. Used for lean-to tents.

A category becomes an annex category by being named in some annexes list. Annex pieces without a door count as open. How annexes are placed is on Annexes.

Tips

  • Start from a shipped piece of similar size and change it. The editor's template list works the same way.
  • Keep one symbol per meaning. Legend symbols are case sensitive, so a and A are two entries.
  • Use . for empty cells, not spaces. Both work, but dots keep rows readable and equally long.
  • Give every Fill and Object anchor a rotated placeholder in things.
  • A gatehouse needs the category WallGateHouse, the type WallPart, two Wall anchors and one Outside anchor.
  • Mark a cell that belongs to the piece but should keep the map's own ground with the floor CBF_EmptyCell. It is not placed in the game.
  • Use Check in the piece editor to test a piece for errors.
  • Loading errors and skipped pieces are written to the log once per piece.
