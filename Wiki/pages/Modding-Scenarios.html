Field reference / Modding-Scenarios

Starting in a ruin

Modding

A scenario can let the colony start in the ruins of a settlement. CBF builds a whole settlement type on the starting map, damages it with a RuinDef and hands the buildings over to the player. The colonists arrive next to it and can claim and repair what is left. Sleeping enemies inside and wreckage in front of the walls are optional.

CBF ships two scenarios that use this:

  • Ruins of a Town (CBF_Scenario_RuinModern) in 1.6/Defs/CustomBaseFramework/Scenario/CBF_Scenario.xml
  • Ruins of a Village (CBF_MO_Scenario_RuinMedieval) in 1.6/Mods/MedievalOverhaul/Defs/Scenarios/CBF_MO_Scenario.xml, only with Medieval Overhaul

Both use the ruin CBF_Ruin_Abandoned, which is defined in the first file.

What happens at game start

  1. After the starting site is chosen, an extra page asks for the settlement type. The player picks one or leaves it random. The map size can be raised on the same page. It cannot go below what the settlement type needs.
  2. CBF builds the settlement on the starting map in the style of a faction that uses this settlement type. Terrain is prepared for it the same way as for a normal settlement.
  3. The ruin is applied: breaches, collapsed roofs, burnt rooms, corpses, plants growing through, and whatever else the RuinDef sets.
  4. All buildings lose their owner, so the player can claim them. Residents are removed and their animals become wild.
  5. The colonists start just outside the settlement.
  6. If set, wreckage is scattered in front of the settlement and a group of sleeping enemies is placed inside.

A start ruin never gets loot and never gets defenders. lootFactor, lootWithOccupiers, prisoners, occupation and occupationChance of the RuinDef have no effect here. They only matter for quest sites.

The list of settlement types only shows types that can be used and that at least one faction in the game uses. If the scenario's player faction has a tech level, only types of that tech level are offered. A medieval player faction gets medieval settlements, a modern one gets modern settlements.

Make your own ruin

A ruin is a CustomBaseFramework.RuinDef. Every field is optional and starts at "no damage". Copy the shipped one and change the numbers:

<CustomBaseFramework.RuinDef ParentName="CBF_Ruin_Basis">
  <defName>MyMod_Ruin_Burnt</defName>
  <label>burnt down</label>
  <wallDemolition>0.2</wallDemolition>
  <breaches>1~3</breaches>
  <gateDestroyed>1</gateDestroyed>
  <buildingDamage>0.3~0.7</buildingDamage>
  <burnMarks>0.8</burnMarks>
  <rubble>0.4</rubble>
  <residentCorpses>3~8</residentCorpses>
  <rotDays>10~40</rotDays>
  <fieldsRavaged>1</fieldsRavaged>
  <livestockGone>1</livestockGone>
  <roofCollapse>0.5</roofCollapse>
  <lightsOut>true</lightsOut>
  <occupation>None</occupation>
</CustomBaseFramework.RuinDef>

The fields that make a settlement look abandoned rather than freshly raided:

Field Meaning
overgrowth Chance per open cell of the settlement that a wild plant of the biome grows there, 0 to 1.
overgrowthTrees Share of those plants that are trees instead of bushes, 0 to 1. Default 0.3.
townWallDecay Share of the town wall that has fallen down, 0 to 1.
roofCollapse How likely the roof of a room has caved in, 0 to 1, more often near the attack.
lightsOut true empties all fuel and switches off everything that can be switched.
rotDays Long values like 60~200 leave only skeletons.

All other fields are listed in RuinDef. For a start ruin, set occupation to None.

The file goes into your mod's Defs folder like any other def. Your ruin then shows up in the scenario editor.

Add the ruin start to a scenario

In the game

Open the scenario editor, add the part Start in a settlement ruin and set:

  • the settlement type, or random
  • the ruin
  • who sleeps inside: nobody, animals, raiders or both
  • the strength of the sleepers in raid points

A scenario can only have one ruin start.

In your own scenario

Add the part to the parts list of your ScenarioDef:

<li Class="CustomBaseFramework.ScenPart_CBFRuinStart">
  <def>CBF_RuinStart</def>
  <ruin>MyMod_Ruin_Burnt</ruin>
  <sleepers>Raiders</sleepers>
  <sleeperPoints>300</sleeperPoints>
  <wakeRadius>8</wakeRadius>
  <battlefield>
    <li><thing>AncientRustedCar</thing><count>1~3</count></li>
    <li><thing>AncientTank</thing><count>0~1</count></li>
  </battlefield>
  <battlefieldDistance>4~25</battlefieldDistance>
</li>
Field Default Meaning
plan random defName of a SettlementPlanDef. Empty lets the player choose, or picks one at random by tech level.
planLocked false true hides the choice page and always uses plan. Needs plan.
ruin none defName of the RuinDef. Without it the settlement stands undamaged.
sleepers Both Who sleeps inside: None, Animals, Raiders or Both. With Both, 60 % of the points go to raiders and 40 % to predators.
sleeperPoints 200 Raid points for the sleepers. 0 means no sleepers.
wakeRadius 6 Cells. The sleepers wake when a colonist comes this close and can see them. Noise also wakes them.
battlefield empty Things scattered in front of the settlement, each with thing and count as a range.
battlefieldDistance 4~25 Distance from the settlement in cells within which the battlefield things are placed.

The raiders come from a hostile faction whose tech level is not above the player's. Permanent enemies are preferred. The animals are predators that live in the biome. They camp under a roof in the part of the settlement farthest from the map centre.

Battlefield things are placed on open, unroofed ground, not on water and not right next to the colonists' starting spot. Use things that exist for every player of your scenario. If a thing needs another mod, put the scenario into a folder that only loads with that mod (see Your own content mod).

The ScenarioDef files of the two shipped scenarios are a full example, including starting items and the start dialog.

In an existing scenario

To add a ruin start to a scenario of the base game or another mod, patch its parts list. This example adds it to Crashlanded:

<?xml version="1.0" encoding="utf-8"?>
<Patch>
  <Operation Class="PatchOperationAdd">
    <xpath>Defs/ScenarioDef[defName="Crashlanded"]/scenario/parts</xpath>
    <value>
      <li Class="CustomBaseFramework.ScenPart_CBFRuinStart">
        <def>CBF_RuinStart</def>
        <ruin>CBF_Ruin_Abandoned</ruin>
        <sleepers>None</sleepers>
      </li>
    </value>
  </Operation>
</Patch>

The file goes into your mod's Patches folder. For a scenario from another mod, wrap the operation in a PatchOperationFindMod so the patch only runs when that mod is loaded. More on this in XML patches.

The patch changes the scenario for everyone who has your mod. If players should be able to choose, make your own scenario instead: copy the original ScenarioDef, give it a new defName and label, and add the part there.

Check your work

  • Start a new game with the scenario and look at the extra page after site selection. If no settlement type is listed, no faction in the game uses one of the matching tech level.
  • If the starting map has no settlement, the log says why. Look for lines starting with CBFStart or Startruine.
  • Errors in your RuinDef or in the scenario part are listed in the log when the game loads, for example a range whose minimum is above its maximum.
