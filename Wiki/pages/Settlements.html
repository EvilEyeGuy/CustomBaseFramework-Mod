Settlements

A settlement plan describes one kind of place: a market town, a fortress city, a village, a camp. The generator builds it fresh for every faction base, so no two look the same.

  1. List grouped by tech level. The number is the weight, a red row has an error that stops the plan from being built.
  2. Preview. Off by default, because planning a large town takes a few seconds. Show preview draws the plan on a flat map, Reroll tries a new seed.
  3. General: name, kind, tech levels, factions and more.
  4. Wards on the right: one tab per ring of the settlement.
  5. Faction overview below the list shows which faction gets which settlement, see Factions.

Settlement types from other mods are read only. Copy into my mod at the bottom puts a copy into your mod and opens it. Build and the preview still work on the original.

Loot of a built settlement

After Build, the button Show loot at the bottom shows what the loot of the settlement built last is worth in silver: in total, per ward and per kind of goods. The value is counted while the settlement is built and saved with it. Medieval Overhaul containers that only fill when searched are not included.

What the generator handles by itself

  • Buildings that belong at the wall, such as barracks, dock onto the town wall. The wall stays and the building gets a door onto the walkway.
  • An open side of such a building that does not touch the wall is closed with the building's own wall.
  • Wall parts cut off by a mountain turn into rock. Large rock pockets behind the wall get a postern. Small rock islands that touch the planned wall stay on the wall line and outside the town and replace the wall there; only the part inside the town is removed.
  • Rubble inside the settlement is cleared.
  • Modern towns always have wards shaped like a spider web: 7 to 10 corners with sagging sides, also with a sandbag or barricade line. They never get gatehouses; roads run through gaps.
  • Medieval settlements at the coast or on a wide river get a harbor with jetties, fishing huts, harbor store, harbor master and boat sheds, linked to the town by a path. The settlement type lists the harbor buildings under hafen, see Settlement types in XML.
  • Buildings whose graphic only has one direction, such as Medieval Overhaul carts, are never rotated.
  • Stoves, ovens and workbenches face into the room, and every thing gets a stuff it can be made of.

General

  • Label: the name players see.
  • Kind of place: medieval town, modern town or camp. Click to switch.
  • Weight: how often this plan is drawn against other plans of the same faction.
  • Material list: what the generator uses for walls, doors, gates, fences, roads and roofs it builds itself, see Material lists. Required; the row is red without one.
  • WD tier: only with World Domination 2.0 active. A dropdown with the tiers of World Domination, a single tier or a range such as Tier 1 to 2. The tier this plan stands for, see Compatibility.
  • WD type: only with World Domination 2.0 active. The World Domination settlement types this plan stands for, such as mining camp or slave village. Only the types of the chosen tier are offered; empty means any type.
  • Tech levels: which ages the plan fits. Click switches one level, right-click picks this level and all above. Pawn lists use their members of the highest tech level at or below the settlement's level.
  • For all factions of these levels: the plan serves every faction of a ticked tech level, unless that faction has plans of its own.
  • Factions: factions or faction categories that get this plan in any case.
  • Excluded: factions that never get it.
  • Mods: the mods the plan needs. Most are found automatically, + by hand adds more.
  • World map icon: the symbol of this place on the world map, tinted in the faction color.

Wards

A ward is one ring: the old town behind the high wall, the suburb behind a lighter one, the countryside around them. The innermost ward is protected best and filled first.

Fortification

  • Walls: the wall pieces of this ring. Several entries give a wall that varies along its length.
  • Emplacements: towers and other works in the ring. The generator spaces them itself, corners first.
  • Gatehouses: built wherever a road crosses the wall.
  • Gate: the thing that closes the passages. Empty uses the gate of the material list.

Modern towns and the countryside have no fortification.

Garrison

Pawn lists that defend this ward, with a count and a post: Inside, Wall or Gate. The editor shows the point value. A ward with a garrison ignores the defender points of the settlement type; without entries the ward takes its share of them.

How the defenders behave:

  • Every ward defends itself. Defenders do not gather in the center and do not storm out together.
  • Guards on the wall and at the gates hold their spot, shooters stay at the embrasures.
  • Crews of ballistas, catapults and trebuchets stay at their gun, even when many defenders have fallen.
  • Defenders of a settlement cannot be recruited as prisoners.

Defences

Defences spread over the whole ward: turret posts, sandbag nests, bunkers. Each entry names a category or one piece and a count. The first one stands at a random spot along a road; every further one goes where it is farthest from those already placed. Use districts instead when a defence belongs to one district only.

Districts

A district is a building programme. Districts without a name of their own are numbered, District 1, District 2 and so on.

  1. Buildings: a category or one specific piece, with min, max and weight. Filler entries fill the space left at the end.
  2. Field: a crop plot grown around the buildings named under Farmsteads. The plants come from single plants or from a Crop list, see Loot.
  3. Pen: animals from a pawn list, the herd size, and what grows inside.
  4. Location of the district: how strongly the district wants to lie near the center, the gate, the wall or the main road, 0 to 1 each. All 0 means a random spot.

+ Building, + Field and + Pen add rows. The ... button next to a category shows the pieces it covers.
